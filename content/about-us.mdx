Welcome to the Spice and Wellness Society at UNC Chapel Hill! We are a student-led organization dedicated to exploring and promoting the rich traditions of South Asian culture for health and wellness. Our mission is to dive into common health issues prevalent in the South Asian community, like heart health and diabetes, and aid in combating them through cultural practices and daily habits. Through the potential of traditional spices and ingredients, we aim to teach our members how to cook healthy dishes while exploring the broader benefits of food for health and well-being. Additionally, we work to debunk common food myths, delve into South Asian cultural practices, and promote mental health.

## A Quick History of South Asian Natural and Cultural Practices

South Asian culture has a long history of utilizing natural ingredients and traditional practices to promote health and wellness. Ayurveda, one of the world’s oldest holistic healing systems, originated in India over 3000 years ago[^saini]. This practice emphasizes the use of spices, herbs, and other natural ingredients in daily life to maintain balance in the body and mind. Similarly, practices such as yoga and meditation, which also have their roots in South Asia, are widely recognized for their benefits and reducing stress and promoting mental clarity[^saini].

Spices like turmeric , cumin, and ginger are not only culinary staples in South Asian households, they have also been used for centuries for their medicinal properties. For example, turmeric is known for its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects, while ginger is often used to aid digestion and combat nausea[^kumar]. These ingredients, along with many others, form the foundation of South Asian wellness practices that continue to be relevant and beneficial today.

## Why This is Relevant

UNC Chapel Hill is a diverse and vibrant community where students come together from various backgrounds. Our organization helps foster a holistic approach to well-being, promotes inclusivity, and celebrates cultural diversity.
